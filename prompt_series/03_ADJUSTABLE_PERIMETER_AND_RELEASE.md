# 03 — Adjustable pass-through frame, gravity loading and controlled release

Build primary mechanism on top of calibrated phone spine. Two parallel captive rails, or keyed anti-rotation beam with evidence, pass through jaws/spine without blocking minimum width. Preferred fixed-left datum jaw and moving right jaw on dual rails, with positive lock; consider both jaws independently sliding if useful for balance, but preserve a reproducible anchor. At W=25.4, 50.8, 152.4, 254 mm ensure the usable opening actually matches label dimensions, not outer frame size; jaws cannot collide with phone mount, screws, stops or each other. Height H=12.7, 50.8, 152.4 mm: both side lips have fixed nominal height 50.8 mm (2 inches) from the bottom seating datum, independent of label height. Parameter name side_lip_height_mm=50.8; no vertical adjustment or telescoping extensions in baseline. Open top at every width; lower datum supports flexible label along enough of its width to avoid sag, but does not trap adhesive. Short labels must remain accessible from the open face and not be buried in deep slots; tall labels extend above the side lips, up to 101.6 mm unsupported. Test toppling, buckling and skew through liner removal and release; if retention is insufficient, investigate temporary nonadhesive face support rather than automatically lengthening lips. No top crossbar. Evaluate tall/narrow buckling and max-width cantilever deflection, eccentric grip forces and off-center phone. Design modular bottom rail support, continuous bar or two datum seats plus removable/captive mid support if justified; maintain bottom reference straight. Captive thumbscrews/cam levers with positive lock, hard end stops and loss prevention; scale graduation optional, never substitute for actual ruler tape. Avoid exposed sharp rail ends; protrusion at min width can snag, use rounded captive caps, shorten/store rails by approved method or split design. Verify full insertion/removal path and no FOD-prone pieces.

The label is a thin flexible sticker, not a rigid card. Design a reproducible loading/application sequence before designing retention: load with liner intact from above, hold by nonadhesive face/perimeter, peel liner via accessible tab while retaining label without touching adhesive; or use tested split-liner method if available. Do not require changing actual label/liner unless explicitly approved. Consider retaining clips that retract sideways/down before pressure or front-only compliant tabs that can swing away while maintaining position. Avoid fixed back lips beneath exposed adhesive; if retracting supports causes falling or skew, provide temporary front retention or compliant finger pad on label face. Open center for direct thumb pressure from mechanic toward wall; design edge retainers to withdraw without crossing freshly bonded surface. Standoff pads hold adhesive clear during alignment; initial adhesion near center or approved edge per label instructions; fixture removed normal to surface without scraping or peeling. Design travel stops, release synchronization or clear sequential releases, pinch clearance, safe handling and no uncontrolled spring ejection. No electrical actuators in V1. Model at least two release options and compare using paper labels before selecting. If a true perimeter-only holder cannot handle thin stickers after liner removal, explicitly state limitation and develop alternative removable face retention, not pretend success.

Provide a fixed lower-left index calibrated to the bottom datum, visible with phone upright. Derive bottom-left placement from top-left engineering target plus known label height (e.g. 22 in + 2 in = 24 in down for a 2-inch-high label, assuming the defined vertical coordinate). If a top-left pointer is beneficial, make it an optional separate targeting aid, not a telescoping retaining lip; account for its offset and clearance. Model left edge and bottom alignment datums, label plane, standoff and contact pads. Include provision for liner retrieval and accountability. Prototype in modular components with captive hardware. For bed size <=220 mm, 254+ mm rails may require standard stock rails or modular keyed joints; do not silently shorten usable travel. Compare inexpensive commercial rods vs printed joint, prefer straight continuous stock if needed; check support and tolerances. All parts use clear names, revisions, mm. Provide rail length formula covering opening + jaw width + travel + end stops. Run min/max and intermediate assembly configurations, swept collision tests, release position checks, center access, lock reach and top-loading checks. Produce individual CAD files/exports plus assembly renders and test coupons.
