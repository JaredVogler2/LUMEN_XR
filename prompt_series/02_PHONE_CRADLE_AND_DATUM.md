# 02 — Portrait phone mount, datum and main spine

Generate editable solids and source, not a visual mockup. Make phone mount removable from the structural spine using a positive latch/dovetail or captive fastener with anti-rotation keys. Do not depend on MagSafe, adhesive, friction alone or force on buttons. Design portrait cradle around parametric phone dimensions and case, with bottom/upper corner pockets and controlled compliant snap hooks or replaceable retention clips. Protect screen and camera, permit insertion/removal without bending phone or stressing glass. Buttons must be accessible and never pressed by clamp; include port/speaker access, front screen visibility, camera bump clearance. A separate dummy phone, envelope keep-outs and fit coupon must be produced. Check different phone dimensions only as labeled test variants. Use gentle radii, generous root fillets on clips, avoid brittle snap hook geometry; print coupons and tune. Do not apply load through bare phone back or camera; load path through chassis and three or more nonmarring contact pads, with pressure distributed on mock wall. Pads replaceable but retained, not loose consumables. Design standoff plane to allow alignment before adhesive touches; do not drag along wall. Provide removable/contact-compatible padding for mock only, no material approval assumptions.

Make datum rail rigid and repeatable relative to phone short axis (X), independent of screen auto-rotate. Provide machined/printed straight reference edge or replaceable straightedge insert for floor tare, usable with fixture empty and in same azimuth as target placard. Design for fixture datum in contact with representative floor reference without phone glass contact. Include calibration offsets, axis arrows, fixed lower-left datum, datum fiducials and top-left pointer location. Pointer must account for height and standoff parallax; use nonmarring line-of-sight index or removable captive mechanical pointer; never obscure adhesive area. Include camera opening if useful but camera optional. Provide explicit mounting transform phone-to-fixture, drawing and datum chain, flatness/parallelism inspection method, anti-loosen feature. Mark orientation arrows. Avoid nominal claims that printed edges are calibrated; show how measured shim or software offset can correct fixed error, not backlash.

Parameterize cradle gap, wall thickness, snap hook deflection, root fillets, pad thickness, spine stiffness, mounting fit, dummy envelope. Initial FDM clearances 0.2–0.4 mm per sliding side only as coupon trial values; choose after fit tests. Use PETG as mock baseline (PLA fit coupons only where appropriate), consider TPU pad/clip only after bench test. Print layer orientation to reduce hook delamination. Do not assume material approved for aircraft. Export cradle, spine, dummy phone, clip and coupon separate. Render portrait front/rear/side, exploded view, camera/button cutaway, assembly dimensions. Validate solids, no intersecting keep-outs, positive retention, full screen view, phone insertion path. Model a fail-safe mechanical tether point only if suitable and explicitly not approved for site use.

## Mandatory top-edge saddle refinement (supersedes full-length cradle preference)
Design the primary mount as an inverted U-shaped saddle over the upper edge of a portrait phone, centered under the horizontal placard rail. The mount must seat fully down onto two widely spaced coplanar rigid datum lands on the phone/case top edge; its rail datum is nominally parallel to the phone short axis. Leave no avoidable gap between saddle datum and top edge, but allow designed relief for rounded corners, protective raised rims and top geometry. Flush means repeatable seating at datum lands, not forced contact across an irregular case or zero clearance everywhere. Use rigid datum lands with thin protective noncompressible or characterized liners, not thick compressible foam as angular reference. The contact interface must not scratch or concentrate excessive load; qualify material and contact force on dummy devices first.

Design thickness adjustment independently along Z (front/back) so it does not change Y seating height, X angular datum or rail-to-phone orientation. Preferred fixed rear reference pad plus captive sliding front jaw, guided against rocking and tightened by a captive thumb screw or low-force cam with hard stop and compliant protective pad. Evaluate front lip engagement only on structurally suitable bezel/case features, never press active glass, front sensor, camera or button. Use geometric positive capture (e.g. suitable case-edge hooks or captive wraparound keeper) independent of friction alone; if compact top saddle cannot safely retain a bare/rounded device, provide model-specific inserts or alternate full cradle and explicitly report limitation. No magnets alone, adhesive on phone, drilling or modification. Adjustable lateral stops or interchangeable keyed inserts may accommodate widths; independently clear top side buttons and rear camera island. Phone removal shall be intentional, tool-free if feasible, and not risk sudden release. Keep screen readable and permit phone controls. Do not allow bare phone or camera island to touch panel; separate structural standoffs set contact plane and transfer force from applicator independently of phone. Adjust or configure standoff depth for case/camera geometry without changing datum relationship; verify placard adhesive can reach wall while phone remains protected.

Expose phone_width_mm, phone_total_thickness_mm, saddle_depth_mm, jaw_travel_mm, datum_land_spacing_mm, top_edge_radius_mm, case_lip_height_mm, camera_keepout, pad_thickness_mm and measured phone_to_fixture angular correction. Test generic widths 65/75/85 mm and thicknesses 8/12/16/20 mm (exploratory, not promised compatibility); avoid falsely claiming all combinations fit. Print a short saddle test coupon and dummy rounded/cased envelopes before printing full rails. Test reseating, clamp force, slip/rotation, rail loads at max extension, case compliance, repeated insertion, screen/button/camera clearance and release. Render side cross-sections at min/max thickness and top datum seating detail. Quantify angular variation from unequal datum compression, case taper and yaw; re-tare with fixture assembled for each phone/case change and calibrate rail-to-phone axis. A case is not automatically a calibrated straightedge. For 50 mm datum spacing, 0.1 mm unequal seating corresponds to about 0.115 degrees; design and test accordingly. Preserve two-inch fixed vertical lips and horizontal pass-through jaws. If a mount cannot be secured with repeatable angular registration, do not mark compatible.
